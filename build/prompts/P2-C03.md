## Executed before writing (the project's BC-2 discipline)

Full-scale run (64 starts/region, full horizons, δ₀ = 1e-6, seed
20260825) on the two worlds as built, *before* any threshold below was
chosen:

| World / region | median sep at step 0 → H | sep(H)/sep(H/2) | max drift |
|---|---|---|---|
| LV / training (H=700) | 1.0e-6 → 1.0e-6 (flat, oscillating) | 0.99 | see below |
| LV / out-high-amplitude | 2.2e-6 → 7.4e-7 (flat, oscillating) | 0.68 | 4.2e-8 (rel) |
| Pendulum / training (H=5000) | 5.3e-8 → 1.0e-7 | 1.37 | 8.9e-12 (rel) |
| Pendulum / out-near-inverted | 4.5e-7 → 5.9e-2 | **405** | 2.0e-7 (rel) |

Pendulum regime check: separation at half-horizon is ~2000× larger
near-inverted than in training — TC-WD4-02's "≥5×" holds by a wide
margin. Pendulum runtime ≈ 35 s per region.

**Two findings that change what the code can honestly assert:**

1. **LV's separation does not "grow roughly linearly" over the
   declared 700-step horizon — it is flat.** LV orbits are neutrally
   stable (V is conserved), so a nearby orbit neither converges nor
   diverges; the separation oscillates around its initial value. The
   linear growth ADR-W3 describes ("linear-in-phase") is real but is a
   slow phase drift between two orbits of slightly different period:
   measured over 7,000 steps (~20 cycles) the median separation grew
   3.7× with log-separation rising slowly and steadily — linear, not
   exponential, exactly the mechanism the spec names, but invisible
   above the within-orbit oscillation at H=700. **The honest TC-WD4-01
   assertion is therefore *sub-exponential*: bounded growth over the
   declared horizon (no exponential runaway), and linear-not-
   exponential over a long horizon.** The spec's "grows roughly
   linearly" wording is imprecise for LV at its own declared horizon —
   recorded in `build/spec-corrections-backlog.md` (A6).

2. **"Relative drift" is ill-defined for LV.** TC-WD3-03 pins "1e-6
   (relative)". LV's invariant V(x,y) crosses zero inside the training
   box (min |V| over the 64 starts = 8.2e-4), so drift divided by |V₀|
   reaches **1.6e-6 — above the bound — while the absolute drift is
   2.2e-9** and the pendulum, whose energy is bounded away from zero,
   is unaffected. Dividing by a quantity that passes through zero is
   not a measurement, it is an artefact of the normaliser. The reason
   the bound exists (ADR-W1: "so that JU-6's conditioned climatology
   cannot be corrupted by integrator error") is about the invariant's
   *binning*, whose natural unit is the invariant's dynamic range over
   the region. **This chunk defines `conserved_rel_drift_max` as
   max|ΔV| / span(V over the region's 64 starts)** — 6.7e-9 for LV
   training, well inside the bound — and states the definition in the
   artefact and the module docstring. Recorded as backlog A7; the
   literal |V₀|-relative form is also computed and reported in the
   artefact (`conserved_rel_drift_max_vs_initial`) so nothing is hidden.

## Scope decisions

- **The climatology reference table** (200,000-step run, 16 bins,
  worlds §5's second artefact) is *not* this chunk: the implementation
  guide's P2-C03 line names only the divergence curves and drift, and
  P4-C05 ("Climatology + horizons") is the consumer that will demand
  the table. `harness.benchmarks` grows that producer then.
- **Runtime vs the unit suite.** A full-scale benchmark is ~80 s.
  Unit tests exercise the real code at reduced scale (`n_starts=8`,
  shortened horizon parameters); one full-scale gate under
  `tests/gates/` is marked `slow` and asserts the spec's actual numbers
  (64 starts, full horizons, the 1e-6 bound, the ≥5× regime factor).
  `pyproject.toml` gains the `slow` marker registration.
- **No file writes.** `harness.benchmarks.build_divergence_artefact()`
  returns the dict; `out/benchmarks/` caching is reporting's write
  (design-review-008 C8), wired at P6-C01.

## Deliverables

1. `src/wmj/worlds/divergence.py` — `perturb(state, delta0)`,
   `separation_curve(transition, state0, horizon, scale, delta0)`,
   `median_separation_curve(curves)`, `conserved_drift(transition,
   conserved, state0, horizon)` (max absolute drift + initial value),
   plus `DriftBoundError` and `assert_drift_within_bound(...)`.
2. `src/wmj/harness/benchmarks.py` — `sample_region_starts(rng, box,
   n)`, `build_divergence_artefact(world_name, world, seeds, n_starts=64,
   horizon=None, delta0=1e-6, drift_bound=1e-6)` returning the worlds §5
   dict (`world`, `perturbation`, `distance`, `regions{name:{steps,
   median_separation}}`, `drift{...}`, `seed`, `n_starts`), raising
   `DriftBoundError` loudly if the bound is violated.
3. Tests: `tests/unit/worlds/test_divergence.py`,
   `tests/unit/harness/test_benchmarks.py`,
   `tests/gates/test_benchmark_full_scale.py` (slow).
4. `pyproject.toml`: `markers = ["slow: ..."]`.

## Review Criteria

Tier 1: Fagan (every gate has a can-fail case — the drift negative,
the regime negative); Keeling (ADR-W3's declared quantities cited
exactly; the two deviations above stated, not smuggled). Tier 3:
Hairer/Nørsett/Wanner (drift is expected and measured, not eliminated);
Goldberg (a median, not a mean, across heavy-tailed chaotic
separations; a normaliser that passes through zero is not a
measurement).
