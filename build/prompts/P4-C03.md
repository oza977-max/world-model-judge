# Build Prompt: P4-C03 — Calibration and sharpness

**In plain words:** two honest questions about a model's stated uncertainty, always answered
together. *Calibration*: when the model says "90% sure the answer is in here", is it in there
about 90% of the time — checked at four confidence levels, 50/80/90/95%? *Sharpness*: how
narrow are those ranges? A model that says "somewhere between zero and a million" is
perfectly calibrated and useless, so the judge never reports one without the other. Both are
measured at one chosen step per task: the step at which the world itself stops being
predictable to that task's tolerance (the "switch step"), or the end of the task's horizon if
it never does.

## Chunk Summary
From `specs/implementation-guide.md` v2.0 P4-C03 and judge spec ADR-J2 (coverage at four
levels with fixed z constants; joint coverage plus per-dimension; one entry per (task, region)
at the task's single evaluation horizon `h_task`), ADR-J3 (sharpness = mean 90% width, same
step), ADR-J5 (the switch step), §5 (blocks and keying). Tests: TC-JU4-01 (calibration and
the summary score are distinct fields; fixed z constants), TC-JU5-01 (tight beats always-wide
at equal calibration), TC-JU5-02 (width is monotone in the stated spread).

## Conventions pinned here (also backlog A31)
- **`h_task`.** ADR-J2/J4 define it as the task's switch step. The switch step is the first step
  `s` in `1..task.horizon` where the region's divergence curve exceeds the task tolerance
  (strictly: a distance equal to the tolerance still passes, worlds §4.1). ADR-J4 does not say
  what `h_task` is when there is no switch step; this build uses the task's own horizon
  (ADR-J5: such a task "is graded by trajectory error for its entire horizon"). The helper
  `switch_step` lives in `climatology.py` (ADR-J5's module) and is reused by P4-C04/C05.
- **Step indexing:** step `s` is array index `s − 1` (see A30).
- **Coverage is closed:** an outcome exactly `z·σ` from the mean is inside (matches the closed
  tolerance rule). The joint coverage counts a trial only if *every* quantity is inside;
  `per_dimension` is four rows (one per level) of per-quantity coverages.
