## What this chunk delivers

In plain words: two numbers that let the judge tell *the model is
wrong* apart from *the world is unpredictable*. First, a **divergence
benchmark** — start two copies of a world a hair apart (δ₀ = 1e-6,
relative) and watch how fast they drift, 64 seeded starts per declared
region, the per-step median RMS-normalised separation. That curve is
the "you can't blame the model for this" floor every error chart is
read against. Second, a **conserved-quantity drift check** — the world
has an invariant (LV's V, the pendulum's energy) that a perfect
integrator would hold constant; we measure how far it actually moves
over a full rollout and gate it against the 1e-6 bound, loudly.

- `src/wmj/worlds/divergence.py` — `perturb` (sign-alternating +,−,+…
  relative perturbation, ADR-W3), `separation_curve`,
  `median_separation_curve`, `conserved_drift`,
  `assert_drift_within_bound` (uses `not (rel < bound)` so a NaN

- `src/wmj/harness/benchmarks.py` — `sample_region_starts`,

## Two spec deviations, recorded — not silent weakenings

Both were executed-first and are in the Round-9 backlog:

- **A6 — LV separation is flat, not "roughly linear," at H=700.** LV
  orbits are neutrally stable, so a nearby orbit neither converges nor
  diverges over the declared horizon (the linear phase-drift the spec
  names only shows over ~20 cycles). The test asserts what is
  observable: bounded / sub-exponential, now **two-sided**
  (`0.1 < ratio < 10.0`) so a broken integrator collapsing the twin
  trajectories toward each other is caught too, not just an exponential
  runaway (pass-1 Minor #3).
- **A7 — drift normaliser.** LV's invariant crosses zero inside the
  training box, so dividing by |V₀| is not a measurement. The artefact
  normalises by the invariant's **span** across all benchmark starts
  and reports the literal relative-to-initial figure alongside as
  `conserved_rel_drift_max_vs_initial` (transparency only), guarded to
  emit `None` rather than crash if every start had a zero invariant
  (pass-1 Minor #5).
