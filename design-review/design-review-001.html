World Model Judge — Design Review 001

Deterministic harness for evaluating learned simulators — design review assessment of seven technical specification documents.

Verdict

Verdict
Do not build

From the seven tech specs as currently written: 10 Critical findings, 16 Important findings, well past the "Not ready" threshold (any Critical, or >6 Important). This is Round 1's expected and valuable outcome: every finding below is cheaper to fix in a spec paragraph than in code, and none of them touches a settled scope decision — they are gaps in the specs' own internal consistency, not requirements problems.

Executive Summary

Four panels (A: Requirements Coverage, B: Interface Contracts, C: Structural Soundness, D: Implementability) each scanned all seven tech-spec documents for one defect class, in parallel, with no shared context. 45/45 requirements have some design representation (Panel A found zero requirements entirely Missing) — but three defects were independently rediscovered by two or more panels working blind to each other, which is the strongest possible signal they are real: the is_fixture field's conflicting sources of truth, the WD-3 integrator gate's inapplicability to any model actually being built, and the LV state-floor clamp-handling contradiction.

Capture-recapture estimate (Lincoln-Petersen, averaged across panel pairs with overlap): ~121 total defects estimated in the population; 33 unique defects found this round → ~27% coverage. This is below the 80% threshold the methodology uses to decide whether a second round is warranted — largely because the four panels' defect classes were so orthogonal that overlap counts are small (1–2 shared findings per pair), which makes the Lincoln-Petersen estimate itself statistically unstable (three pairwise estimates ranged from 76 to 187). Read this as "the panels barely stepped on each other's toes, so we don't have strong evidence about what's left, not proof that ~90 more defects exist." A second liberal round is defensible if you want higher confidence before build, but given the cost concern already raised this session, my recommendation is: fix the findings below (which already give the build team a much sounder base), then move to build and let /gvm-code-review catch what a design review at this depth can't see — rather than spending another quick-scan round chasing a statistically shaky estimate.

Critical Findings

C1 — is_fixture has conflicting producers

found independently by Panels B and C

models.md says is_fixture never enters JudgeInput and is rejoined only at reporting via a harness-owned side map — but judge.md's own Verdict schema (which verdict.py is said to assemble) includes "is_fixture": false as a top-level field, and reporting.md is told to treat both the Verdict and the side map as sources for it, with no rule for which wins if they disagree. This sits directly on the JU-1 blindness guarantee.

Fix: Remove is_fixture/model_ref/meta from the judge-owned Verdict; have the harness wrap it in an outer envelope (JudgedResult{verdict, model_ref, is_fixture, meta}) so there is exactly one producer of each fact.

C2 — Verdict's meta block requires exactly the ambient access JU-12 forbids

Panel C

meta.platform and meta.prereg_commit are environment/filesystem reads, assigned by cross-cutting's own ADR-002 to "orchestration and reporting layers, at the edges" — not to verdict.py, which imports only numpy, math, dataclasses, typing. As written, either the judge is smuggling in ambient access (violating JU-12) or an unspecified second assembly stage patches the "pure" Verdict after the fact (making JU-9's "one structured record" claim untrue). Also duplicates run-manifest.json's content with no single source of truth.

Fix: Move meta out of the judge-produced Verdict entirely into the harness-owned envelope/manifest.

C3 — Chart 1 (the primary chart, RP-1) needs data the Verdict schema doesn't carry, and three of its own drawing rules are undefined

Panel B found the missing data; Panel D independently found three sub-issues in the same chart

Reporting.md declares the exception plot scatters all 200 individual trial outcomes and bands, but its own stated inputs are "Verdict records only" — and the Verdict's exceptions block contains only aggregate counts, no per-trial array. Separately and independently, Panel D found: (a) no rule for collapsing a multi-dimensional per-dimension prediction into the single scalar Y-value and band the chart draws; (b) JU-8 defines its 200 trials per region, but Chart 1's "200 trials" x-axis doesn't say which region(s) it covers or how; (c) "ordered by start-state position along the region" names no actual projection for a 2–4 dimensional starting box.

Fix: Add a per-trial array (outcome, band_lo, band_hi, is_exception) to the Verdict, or explicitly source Chart 1 from raw harness rollout data instead. Then pin the aggregation rule, the region scope, and the ordering projection explicitly.

C4 — The conditioned-climatology mechanism is underspecified on three independent axes

found by all three of Panels B, C, and D, each from a different angle

Panel B: no spec section anywhere gives the climatology table's actual field names/shape (worlds.md §5, the section reserved for exactly this, only has the divergence-curve JSON). Panel C: no defined behaviour when a compared step's invariant value falls outside the bins built from reference runs (a real case — WD-5's out-of-region starts and JU-6's own action-driven excursions both anticipate exactly this), and no defined behaviour when a task's divergence curve never crosses tolerance (switch_step undefined, breaking JU-7's horizon-capping formula). Panel D: the bin-edge rule (equal-width vs. equal-population) and the reference-run length are both unstated numbers in a document that otherwise pins numbers precisely (N=200, the exact-binomial bands).

Fix: Add the climatology-table JSON contract to worlds.md §5; define out-of-range and no-switch-step behaviour explicitly (worlds.md ADR-W4's boundary-inclusion treatment is the right model to follow); pin the bin-edge method and reference-run length as concrete numbers.

C5 — WD-3's integrator-match gate names a component that doesn't exist in any model actually being built

found independently by Panels C and D

"The world's and the model-rollout driver's integrator are the same function object" (worlds.md) describes a gate that can only compare two integrator-calling code paths — but none of the eight contestants (2 baselines, Model A, Model B, 3 fixtures — models.md ADR-M1–M4) ever call an integrator; they're all direct function approximators of the transition, which is the entire point of a learned world model. TC-WD3-01 as literally written cannot be implemented without inventing a component the rest of the spec suite never mentions.

Fix: Restate WD-3's actual enforceable property — most plausibly that the harness's ground-truth generator uses one rk4_step call site everywhere (training data, benchmarks, evaluation truth), and that models are checked for dt-alignment via their rollout loop's step-count mapping, not "same code object."

C6 — Double pendulum equations of motion are named ("textbook"), not written down

Panel D

The double pendulum has multiple standard parameterizations with different sign conventions on the coupled angular-acceleration terms — physically equivalent, numerically different. WD-3/WD-7 demand byte-identical determinism and TC-WD1-01 requires 15-significant-digit hand-verified reference values; whoever derives those values is silently choosing the convention for the whole project, with nothing else to check against.

Fix: Write out the explicit θ̈₁, θ̈₂ and energy formulas in worlds.md §4.2, or cite one specific unambiguous source with the exact sign convention.

C7 — The standard-normal quantile function (z_p) is used everywhere but never computable from what's specified

Panel D

math.erf gives the forward normal CDF, not its inverse. Four confidence levels (50/80/90/95%) are used repeatedly (ADR-J2/J3, JU-8's 90% interval) — a developer could hardcode the four known z-multipliers instead of implementing a general inverse-CDF, but the spec never says this is the intended approach.

Fix: State explicitly: "z_p for {0.50, 0.80, 0.90, 0.95} are fixed constants: 0.6745, 1.2816, 1.6449, 1.9600," or specify the inversion algorithm if a general one is actually wanted.

C8 — Fixture fx-honest-rough's spread-widening rule is prose, not a formula

Panel D

"Spread honestly widened to match the true resulting error" gives no formula — empirical post-hoc fit, or an analytic combination? Over what dataset, fixed once or per-rollout?

Fix: Give the exact formula (e.g. spread ← sqrt(base_spread² + noise_σ²)) or the precise empirical-fit procedure.

C9 — The implementation guide's own build sequence violates the MVP-1 rule it claims to satisfy

Panel D, MVP-1/Tracer-Bullet Assessment

P1-C02 is a genuine tracer bullet (real world, real baseline, real judge computation, real serialized output). But Phases 2–5 (18 of 23 chunks) build strictly by architectural layer — all of worlds, then all of models, then all of judge, then all of reporting against fixture verdict records, not the real pipeline — with the first (and only) further user-visible capability not landing until chunk 19 of 23 (P6-C01). This is precisely the horizontal-build pattern the gvm-tech-spec skill's own MVP-1 Hard Gate exists to catch, and no exemption was declared for it.

Fix: Either declare and justify an MVP-1 exemption for this project shape, or resequence so at least one more user-visible slice (e.g. "one chart, from real data, for the LV skeleton") lands well before chunk 19.

C10 — MU-6's headline experiment (MU-5's model-training recipe) has four unstated numeric/algorithmic choices that would each produce a different trained model from the same seed

cluster of Panel D findings D5, D6, D7, D8 — grouped because they all threaten the same MU-8/MU-6 determinism-and-comparability guarantee

Weight initialization scheme: unspecified. Model A's Gaussian NLL loss: named, not written out (unlike CRPS, which gets a full closed form). Batching: "full-batch gradient check" vs. "seeded batch shuffling" contradict each other without saying which describes actual training. Adam's ε: never given. Two implementers following this recipe with the same seed would not produce the same trained model — which breaks MU-8's entire premise and puts a question mark over whether the pre-registered MU-5 matching margin was ever measuring a well-defined pair of models.

Fix: Pin all four: initialization formula, exact NLL expression, full-batch-vs-mini-batch (and batch size if mini-batch), and ε — in prereg/recipe.md's described contents (models.md ADR-M5), at the same precision CRPS already gets.

Important Findings (16)

1. Divergence-curve array length/indexing mismatch

worlds.md's artefact is H+1 entries (0-indexed); judge.md's JudgeInput says [H]; error_vs_horizon.steps starts at 1. Three different origins for curves that RP-2 requires "on the same axes." (Panel B)

2. exceptions.per_task has no region field

despite ADR-J4's trials being explicitly per-region, unlike calibration and error_vs_horizon which both carry one. (Panel B)

3. Trust horizon's third unit (natural_units) is in the Verdict schema but never rendered

reporting.md's Chart 4 column spec names only steps + world-time, silently dropping the JU-7-mandated cycle-fraction field from "the headline result." (Panel B)

4. The "one distance everywhere" claim is asserted in the judge spec, never shown

judge.md's own component design never states or cites the ADR-W3 RMS-normalised-distance formula for the "median normalised error" it reports, unlike CRPS which gets a full derivation. (Panel B)

5. No join key between the divergence artefact's named regions and the boolean region-label flags

works today because each world has exactly one out-region, but WD-5 explicitly permits more than one, which would break this silently. (Panel B, borderline)

6. Architecture overview's own conceptual-integrity self-check is factually wrong

"no second metric exists anywhere" is contradicted by the same document set (CRPS for the anti-gaming skill summary vs. RMS distance for horizon/tolerance/divergence) — a defensible design, but the self-audit's absolute claim doesn't survive checking against its own specs. (Panel C)

7. JU-8's hedging cross-flag ("bottom-decile sharpness") has no defined reference population

and with ~8 contestants per (model, world, region), a decile isn't statistically meaningful anyway. (Panel C)

8. Contradictory clamp-event handling

found independently by Panels C and D — worlds.md §4.1 says clamp activations are merely "logged"; §7 says a clamped trajectory in a "benchmark or judged run" aborts loudly. Whether training-data generation counts as one of those scopes, or silently tolerates unphysical clamped states baked into training data, is never stated — directly threatening MU-7/MU-8's honesty guarantees if the wrong reading is implemented.

9. reset()'s cross-rollout isolation is never tested

the exact "model remembers something across rollouts" risk this project's own architecture is designed to prevent (models.md ADR-M1) has no corresponding TC-ID checking it actually holds, which would silently corrupt JU-8's independence assumption if violated. (Panel C, borderline)

10. Pre-registration integrity rests on an unstated trust assumption

"append-only" prereg/ history is a convention, not a technical control; git history is rewritable by the same single author JU-10 already discloses as a limitation. Worth naming alongside JU-10's other disclosures rather than left implicit. (Panel C, borderline)

11. Fixture fx-brittle's "half the training box" is ambiguous

for a multi-dimensional box (2D LV / 4D pendulum) — halve one axis, all axes, or by volume? Changes what the fixture actually demonstrates. (Panel D)

12. Trust horizon has no stated convention for h=1 failing tolerance

"the largest h ≤ switch_step such that error stays within τ" has no solution if h=1 already fails; presumably 0, but never stated. (Panel D, borderline)

13. Φ/φ's array-vectorization strategy over NumPy is unaddressed

and φ is described as derived "from erf" when it's actually from exp — harmless imprecision, but the vectorization question (scalar-only math.erf vs. array-shaped JudgeInput) is a real implementation decision left silent. (Panel D)

14. JU-10's seven limitations disclosures are counted everywhere, authored nowhere

only a placeholder string appears in the Verdict schema; contrast with reporting.md's caption templates, which this project's own pattern says should be authored in the spec, not improvised at build time, specifically because they're credibility-critical. (Panel A)

15. NF-4's forbidden-terms list is referenced by three separate spec sections and declared in none of them

TC-NF4-01 explicitly promises this list will be "declared and kept current in the technical spec"; that promise is unmet. (Panel A)

16. MU-6's prereg/recipe.md doesn't explicitly list the MU-1 uncertainty format as one of its contents

even though MU-6's own text names it — likely satisfied in substance (the format is fixed in cross-cutting.md before any run) but not traceably inside the mechanism the rest of MU-6/JU-11 relies on. (Panel A, borderline)

  • No worked API-contract example for the pendulum's out-of-region key name (only LV's is shown). (Panel B)
  • JU-9's not_tested example list is truncated with "..." rather than fully enumerated — soft gap, JU-9 only requires "an explicit list." (Panel A)
  • SVG chart duplicates and results.html aren't traced to any requirement ID — reasonable engineering choices, but worth a light trace note per this project's own traceability discipline. (Panel A)

  • P1-C02 is labelled "user-facing" but its only output is a JSON file — the project's actual named user (Dev) explicitly reads charts, not source or JSON. More accurately "developer/pipeline-facing." (Panel D)

Cross-Panel Themes

1. The Verdict schema was written before its consumers' exact needs were re-checked against it.

C1, C2, C3, and Important #1–3 are all instances of the same root cause: judge.md's §5 example schema was authored once and never re-verified against what reporting.md's chart designs and cross-cutting's purity rule actually require. A single pass reconciling the Verdict schema against every consumer (reporting.md's four charts, JU-12's purity boundary) would likely resolve 6 of the 26 Critical+Important findings at once.

2. The climatology mechanism (JU-6/ADR-J5) is the single most underspecified part of the whole suite

flagged from three independent angles (missing contract, undefined edge cases, unpinned constants) by three different panels using three different defect-class lenses. This is exactly the "architecturally risky, novel part" Fairbanks' risk-driven principle says deserves the most design depth, and it currently has the least.

3. Numeric/formula precision is inconsistent across the judge spec

CRPS gets a full closed-form derivation; z_p, φ's vectorization, the climatology binning, and Model A's NLL loss do not. The spec's own best moments (CRPS, the exact-binomial band derivation) show the right level of precision is achievable; it just wasn't applied uniformly.

What Prevents a Higher Score

Every dimension above 4 is prevented from a 10 by concrete, named gaps, not structural ceilings — nothing here is "acceptable trade-off," everything is fixable directly in the spec text with no re-architecture required. The Verdict schema (B, C) and the models/judge numeric precision (D) are the two places worth fixing first, since they're both cross-cutting: fixing the Verdict schema once resolves findings in three different specs at once, and pinning the four training constants (C10) resolves the single biggest threat to this project's own headline determinism claim.
