Code review 003 — Phase 4 (the judge)

2026-10-14 to 2026-10-22 · Round 3 (strict) · three calibrated defect-class panels plus one blind panel (a leaner set than the skill's full panel list, to save usage); no slow gates in reviewer runs; Panel F (stub detection) omitted — no STUBS.md. Scope: src/wmj/judge/*, src/wmj/harness/climatology.py, the purity and blindness gates and their tests.

In plain words: the arithmetic of the judge held up. The review's one serious find was a design flaw, not a typo: the rule for "this forecast missed" checked each predicted number separately, so a perfectly honest model that predicts four numbers would have been called overconfident about a third of the time instead of one time in ten. That would have made the judge unfair to exactly the models it is meant to be fair to. It is fixed by asking one question about the whole answer.

Verdict

Merge with caveats. 1 Critical, 2 Important, 13 Minor and 7 Observations. The Critical and both Important findings below were all fixed after your triage and verified by running things (full suite 1,905 passed at the table-units commit; the judge, harness and gate subsets re-run after the minors). The caveats are the open owner decisions D19 and D21, and the deferred door-tightening listed at the end — none of them blocks the next phase.

Honest limits of this review

  • The reviewer panels were fewer than the skill prescribes (three calibrated, one blind) to keep usage lean; a missed class of defect is possible, which is why the purity and blindness gates are mechanical rather than reviewed.
  • The Critical was found by a panel that checked the design against what an honest model must score, not by the fixes' author; the fix itself was re-verified by mutation (ten targeted mutants killed) and by a simulation of an honest model, but the fix-check was not a separate fresh reviewer.

Recurring pattern: a rule that is right for one quantity (the 90% range) was extended to several without re-deriving it — the same shape as the runtime envelope that was estimated instead of measured. Both were found by running the thing.
